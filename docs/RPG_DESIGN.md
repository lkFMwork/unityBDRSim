# RPG Layer — Create-Your-BDR

The call simulator is the "encounter." The RPG layer wraps it so the player owns
a character, makes meaningful build choices, and grows a career over many calls.

## Create your BDR

In the `CharacterCreate` scene you set:

- **Name** — first / last.
- **Sales Style (race)** — one of six styles (Closer, Hunter, Farmer, Challenger,
  Networker, Generalist). Each grants stat modifiers, a passive **trait**, and a
  starting **active ability** — see [`SalesStyleLibrary`](../Assets/Scripts/Data/SalesStyle.cs).
- **Ability scores** — D&D-style **point-buy**: every stat starts low and you
  spend a budget to raise them, with the top tiers costing a premium
  ([`PointBuy`](../Assets/Scripts/Data/PointBuy.cs)). Style modifiers apply on top.
- **Active abilities** — fired mid-call (Anchor High, Reframe, Second Wind, Build
  Rapport), granted by your style and unlocked deeper in the ability trees.
- **Look** — a live 3D avatar (built from primitives) you customize: skin tone,
  outfit, accent, hair color, build, and height. It sways/idles so the 3D reads.

The avatar is generated by [`AvatarBuilder`](../Assets/Scripts/UI/AvatarBuilder.cs).
Its `SetConfig` method is the seam to swap in a real rigged model + Animator later
without changing anything else.

## Attributes → call effects

Attributes run 1–10 (5 = neutral). [`CharacterModifiers`](../Assets/Scripts/Simulation/CharacterModifiers.cs)
turns them into concrete levers on a call — this is what makes a build *matter*:

| Attribute | Effect on a call |
| --- | --- |
| **Charisma** | Higher starting trust; bonus to Rapport scoring |
| **Negotiation** | Rate "headroom" — prospects accept higher rates before balking |
| **Product Knowledge** | Bonus to Value Articulation and Objection Handling scoring |
| **Resilience** | Patience drains slower (you wear down less under pressure) |
| **Prospecting** | Bonus to Discovery scoring |

The effects are deterministic and unit-tested, and they feed directly into the
existing `CallSession` / `NegotiationEngine`.

## Progression

[`ProgressionSystem`](../Assets/Scripts/Simulation/ProgressionSystem.cs) handles
XP, levels, skill points, and career rank:

- **XP per call** = grade (0–100 from the scorecard) + an outcome bonus
  (won a load > trial > follow-up > rejected > hung up).
- **Leveling** uses a rising cost curve; each level grants **2 skill points**.
- **Career rank** climbs with level: BDR → Senior BDR → Account Executive →
  Sales Manager.

Career stats (calls made, deals won, hang-ups, total weekly margin won, win rate)
accumulate on the character and show on the menu.

## Persistence

[`SaveSystem`](../Assets/Scripts/Core/SaveSystem.cs) writes the character to JSON
under `Application.persistentDataPath`. `GameManager` loads it on boot, so the BDR
and their career persist between sessions. "New BDR" on the menu starts over.

## Flow

```
MainMenu ──(no save)──► CharacterCreate ──save──► MainMenu
   │                                                  │
   └──────────── pick scenario ──► CallFloor ─────────┘
                                      │
                              call ends → award XP,
                              update career, save, show
                              grade + level-up
```

## Career loop (Milestone 2)

The menu is now a **career hub**. Play runs on a workweek:

- **Days & calls** — each workday gives a fixed number of calls
  (`CareerSystem.BaseCallsPerDay`, +perks). "Take a Call" generates a fresh
  prospect and consumes one; "End Day" advances the calendar and refills calls.
- **Procedural prospects** — [`ProspectGenerator`](../Assets/Scripts/Simulation/ProspectGenerator.cs)
  builds an endless supply of leads (names, companies, lanes, objections),
  scaling difficulty with your level and the week. Deterministic per seed.
- **Weekly quota** — win a target number of deals per 5-day week. Clearing it
  grants a skill point + bonus XP; missing it just resets. Goals ramp each week.
- **Practice calls** — the three authored scenarios remain available off to the
  side; they grant XP but don't consume calls or count toward quota.

`CareerSystem` is pure logic and unit-tested; the career state is saved with the
character.

## Ability trees & active abilities

Spend skill points (leveling + weekly quotas) in the **Ability Trees** overlay.
There are three trees in [`PerkLibrary`](../Assets/Scripts/Data/PerkDefinition.cs):

- **Rapport** — Silver Tongue → Warm Opener → Trusted Voice (grants *Build Rapport*)
- **Deal-Making** — Closer's Instinct → Anchor Master (grants *Anchor High*) → Rate Defender
- **Hustle** — Thick Skin → Workaholic → Rainmaker (grants *Second Wind*)

Each node sits behind the previous tier (a prerequisite). Nodes are plain numeric
effects that aggregate without special-casing, and some **grant active abilities**.

**Active abilities** ([`AbilityLibrary`](../Assets/Scripts/Data/AbilityDefinition.cs))
are fired during a call from the abilities bar (limited uses per call):

| Ability | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| Anchor High | next rate offer gets +headroom |
| Second Wind | restore prospect patience |
| Reframe | instantly handle the active objection |
| Build Rapport | a burst of trust |

Style traits + tree nodes flow into calls via `CharacterModifiers.FromCharacter`,
into XP via `ProgressionSystem`, and into the daily call budget via `CareerSystem`
— all through a single `PerkSystem.Aggregate`.

## Gatekeeper Gauntlet (verbal combat)

When a meeting has a gatekeeper, you fight your way past them in a turn-based
"verbal duel" before reaching the decision-maker
([`GatekeeperDuel`](../Assets/Scripts/Simulation/GatekeeperDuel.cs)):

- You have **Composure** (HP); the gatekeeper has **Resolve** (HP).
- Each turn the gatekeeper shows a **stance** with a readable **tell**. Pick the
  move it's weak to for a **critical hit** on their Resolve; pick the move it
  resists and it **backfires** onto your Composure; anything else makes some
  progress but takes pushback.
- The four moves key off your stats — **Charm** (Charisma), **Credibility**
  (Product Knowledge), **Urgency** (Negotiation), **Curveball** (Prospecting) —
  and **Resilience** raises Composure and softens hits.
- Win → you're put through (into the call). Lose → they hang up (no meeting).

The stance/weakness mappings mirror real gatekeeper tactics, so it trains while it
entertains. Flow: a gatekeeper scenario routes through the `GatekeeperDuel` scene,
then `CallFloor` on a win.

## Open world — the city (greybox)

From the menu, **Drive the City** loads the `City` scene — a greybox town you
explore to meet clients in person:

- **On foot** (CharacterController) with WASD; the camera chases at a fixed angle.
  Your created avatar is the body.
- **Drive** — walk up to the car and press **F** to get in; **F** again to exit.
- **Interact** — walk/drive up to a building and press **E**. Client sites
  generate a meeting (consuming a career call, routing through the Gatekeeper
  Gauntlet if there's a gatekeeper); the **FitzMark Office** returns you to the hub.
- Meetings return you to wherever you launched them (`GameManager.HubScene`), so
  city meetings come back to the city.

Controllers live in the `Fitzmark.BDRSim.World` assembly; the scene + buildings
are generated by the setup tool (re-run it to get the City scene).

## Roadmap — toward the full open-world title

Built so far: create-a-BDR, stat-driven calls, career loop, skill trees, the
Gatekeeper Gauntlet, and the explorable city. Remaining, in planned phases:

1. **The office interior** — greybox bullpen, meeting room, and break room;
   senior-rep NPCs you talk to for advice and side-quests.
2. **Quest system** — objectives, a quest log + HUD tracker, and rewards that
   thread the world, calls, and duels together.
3. **Promotions & events** — rank-up ceremony, rival/leaderboard, streaks,
   achievements, random events, and "boss" accounts.
4. **AAA presentation** — rigged characters + Animator (the `AvatarBuilder.SetConfig`
   seam is ready), city/office art, VO, music, VFX, and real vehicle physics +
   collisions for the car.

Each phase ships as a self-contained, compiling, greybox-playable slice with clean
seams where real art and audio drop in.
